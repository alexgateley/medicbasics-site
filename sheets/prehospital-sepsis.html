Prehospital Sepsis

Field screening, sepsis alert, fluids, pressors and antibiotics en route under SSC 2026, for EMT, paramedic and critical care transport

Checked against current sources October 2026

Download one-page PDFLetter landscape, ready to print or keep with your notes

Recognize it

Sepsis-3: infection plus life-threatening organ dysfunction from a dysregulated host response. Septic shock: sepsis needing a vasopressor to keep MAP ≥65 mmHg, with lactate >2 mmol/L, despite adequate fluid.

  • Source: lungs, urine, skin, abdomen, lines or devices
  • New confusion, often the only sign in older adults
  • Temp >38 °C or <36 °C; no fever does not rule it out
  • HR >90, RR ≥22, SBP ≤100 or MAP <65
  • Mottled skin, delayed or flash capillary refill, falling SpO₂
  • High risk: older, immunocompromised, recent surgery
SSC 2026, in hospital: NEWS/NEWS2, MEWS or SIRS over qSOFA as a single screening tool

Exam tip New in SSC 2026: use a standard screening tool en route (NEWS2 did best, but none is named). Exam point: qSOFA alone is not a screening tool.

Supporting findings: ETCO₂ ≤25 mmHg suggests metabolic acidosis and poor perfusion; it falls as lactate rises. Point-of-care lactate >2 mmol/L is abnormal.

Sepsis alert and antibiotics

  • Pre-notify early: screen result, vitals, source, fluid given, ETA
  • Hospital goal: antibiotics within 1 hr of recognizing shock or probable sepsis
  • SSC 2026 (new, conditional): antibiotics in the ambulance or aircraft for probable or definite sepsis with hypotension when hospital evaluation is over 60 min away
  • Only with a structured en route screening process (SSC); agent chosen with receiving hospitals (NASEMSO)
  • Draw blood cultures first when it does not delay the dose
Exam tip Exam check: all three are needed (probable sepsis, hypotension, over 60 min). Mostly a critical care transport and protocol topic.

Fluids

  • Balanced crystalloid (lactated Ringer's, Plasma-Lyte) over 0.9% saline
  • Hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L: at least 30 mL/kg in the first 3 hr (80 kg ≈ 2.4 L)
  • Field: rapid boluses (e.g., 500 mL); after each, recheck MAP, mentation, capillary refill, lungs, SpO₂
  • Albumin: SSC 2026 suggests crystalloid alone; case by case after large volumes or in cirrhosis; avoid in TBI
Safety New crackles, falling SpO₂ or harder breathing after a bolus: stop fluid, support breathing, and consider a cardiogenic cause and a vasopressor.

Vasopressors in transport

  • SSC 2026: start peripherally rather than delay for a central line
  • Large vein, ideally at or above the antecubital fossa and short term (SSC 2021); avoid leg veins; check the site often
  • Extravasation: stop the infusion and treat per protocol (phentolamine)
  • Dopamine only if norepinephrine and epinephrine are unavailable; push-dose epinephrine per protocol
Safety Pressor-dependent shock, adrenal insufficiency or long-term steroid use: hydrocortisone per protocol (NASEMSO model: 2 mg/kg, max 100 mg IV/IM). SSC 2026 suggests IV corticosteroids in septic shock, commonly hydrocortisone 200 mg/day.

Children

Phoenix (2024): suspected infection plus a Phoenix Sepsis Score ≥2 (respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation or neurologic); SIRS is retired. Septic shock adds ≥1 cardiovascular point: severe hypotension for age, lactate ≥5 mmol/L, or a vasoactive drug. The score needs labs, so field recognition rests on perfusion and mental status.

  • Tachycardia, tachypnea, abnormal capillary refill, cool or mottled skin, lethargy; infants may be hypothermic
  • Hypotension is late (decompensated shock): SBP <60 in neonates, <70 in infants, <70 + (2 × age) at 1–10 yr, <90 over 10 yr
  • Septic shock: 10–20 mL/kg boluses of balanced crystalloid, up to 40–60 mL/kg in hour 1 where ICU care is available
  • Reassess after every bolus; stop for crackles, hepatomegaly or worse breathing
  • Vasoactive drug peripherally rather than waiting; no epinephrine vs norepinephrine preference
  • Antibiotics within 1 hr for septic shock, ideally within 3 hr for sepsis without shock
